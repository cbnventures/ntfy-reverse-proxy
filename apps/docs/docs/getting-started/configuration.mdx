`config.json` defines the <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">servers</Terminology>, <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">contexts</Terminology>, and global settings that the Cloudflare <Terminology title="The proxy itself, running on your Cloudflare account" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">Worker</Terminology> reads at runtime.

## Overview

The configuration lives in a single `config.json` file managed through the CLI (never edited by hand in production). The file is gitignored — only `config.sample.json` is committed.

The config has three top-level sections: `servers`, `contexts`, and `settings`.

## Settings

| Field                  | Type      | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| :--------------------- | :-------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `worker_name`          | `string`  | Name of the Cloudflare <Terminology title="The proxy itself, running on your Cloudflare account" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">Worker</Terminology>, used in <Terminology title="Generated from config.json for Wrangler to deploy" to="/docs/reference/terminology#cloudflare-setup">wrangler.toml</Terminology> generation. Use lowercase letters, digits, and hyphens, without a hyphen at the start or end.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `base_domain`          | `string`  | The <Terminology title="The domain every context subdomain lives under" to="/docs/reference/terminology#cloudflare-setup">domain</Terminology> under which all <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">context</Terminology> subdomains are registered (e.g., `ntfy.example.com`). Lowercase letters, digits, hyphens, and dots, with at least two labels.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `show_response_output` | `boolean` | When `true`, POST/PUT responses include detailed <Terminology title="Shows what happened inside the proxy, secrets masked" to="/docs/reference/terminology#configuration">debug output</Terminology> (<Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">context</Terminology>, <Terminology title="Turns one app's raw alert into a clean ntfy message" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">interpreter</Terminology>, <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">server</Terminology> results). Set to `false` in production to avoid leaking information. |

:::warning
Keep `show_response_output` set to `false` in production. Enabling it exposes <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">context</Terminology> names, <Terminology title="Turns one app's raw alert into a clean ntfy message" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">interpreter</Terminology> types, and per-<Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">server</Terminology> delivery results in HTTP responses.
:::

## Servers

Each <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">server</Terminology> represents an ntfy instance. <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">Servers</Terminology> are defined once and referenced by name from <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">contexts</Terminology>.

| Field    | Type     | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| :------- | :------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `name`   | `string` | Name for this <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">server</Terminology>, referenced by <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">contexts</Terminology> in `primary_server` and `servers` fields. Keep names unique; the CLI rejects duplicates, but config validation doesn't. |
| `server` | `string` | Full URL of the ntfy <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">server</Terminology> (must start with `https://`).                                                                                                                                                           |
| `token`  | `string` | Access token for the ntfy <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">server</Terminology> (must start with `tk_`). Created via the ntfy CLI.                                                                                                                                 |

```json
{
  "name": "primary",
  "server": "https://ntfy.example.com",
  "token": "tk_your_token_here"
}
```

## Contexts

<Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">Contexts</Terminology> define notification sources. Each webhook source or email sender maps to one <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">context</Terminology>. There are two types: HTTP <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">contexts</Terminology> and email <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">contexts</Terminology>.

### HTTP Context

| Field               | Type       | Required | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| :------------------ | :--------- | :------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `id`                | `string`   | Yes      | Unique identifier that becomes the subdomain in the route (e.g., `{id}.ntfy.example.com`). Use 1–63 lowercase letters, digits, or hyphens; a hyphen cannot be first or last. The CLI generates one when you leave it blank.                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `name`              | `string`   | Yes      | Human-readable name for display in CLI and responses.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `type`              | `"http"`   | Yes      | Must be `"http"` for webhook <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">contexts</Terminology>.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `interpreter`       | `string`   | Yes      | Which <Terminology title="Turns one app's raw alert into a clean ntfy message" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">interpreter</Terminology> to use. One of: `plain-text`, `ntfy-json`, `statuspage`, `synology`, `seerr`, `pfsense`, `unifi`, `transmission-vpn`, `pfsense-actions`, `discord-stonker-bot`, `outpost`, `mailmask`.                                                                        |
| `topic`             | `string`   | Yes      | The ntfy <Terminology title="The ntfy channel your phone subscribes to for alerts" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">topic</Terminology> to publish to.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `error_topic`       | `string`   | No       | Optional ntfy <Terminology title="Where you hear about alerts the proxy couldn't handle" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">topic</Terminology> where authentication and interpretation errors are sent as one-shot JSON debug <Terminology title="A file sent along with the notification" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">attachments</Terminology> outside the durable lane.                   |
| `error_events`      | `string[]` | No       | Filters which authentication and interpretation diagnostics notify `error_topic`. Any of `authentication`, `interpretation`. Omit to notify on both (the default); set to `["interpretation"]` to keep <Terminology title="Turns one app's raw alert into a clean ntfy message" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">interpreter</Terminology>-error alerts while silencing authentication-failure noise. Dropped-notification notices always go to `error_topic` when it is set. |
| `mode`              | `string`   | Yes      | Delivery mode: `"send-once"` or `"send-all"`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `show_visitor_info` | `boolean`  | Yes      | When `true`, appends visitor IP, location, and ISP details to the notification body.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `primary_server`    | `string`   | Yes      | Name of the preferred <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">server</Terminology>, which must match a <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">server</Terminology>'s `name` and appear in `servers`. Tried first in `send-once` mode.                                   |
| `servers`           | `string[]` | Yes      | Ordered list of <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">server</Terminology> names. At least one required.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `token`             | `string`   | No       | Optional authentication token for this <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">context</Terminology>. When set, incoming requests must include `Authorization: Bearer {token}` (or the raw token as the header value).                                                                                                                                     |
| `session` | `object` | No | Turns on <Terminology title="A short-lived, one-use pass a sender gets from /session" to="/docs/reference/terminology#configuration">session tokens</Terminology> for senders whose code is public. Cannot be combined with `token` or `secret_path`; see [Session Tokens](/docs/features/security#session-tokens). |
| `secret_path` | `string` | No | A <Terminology title="An unguessable URL path only your sender knows" to="/docs/reference/terminology#configuration">secret path</Terminology> of 32 to 128 lowercase letters and digits, for senders that can't send headers, such as Statuspage. Requests must use `https://{id}.ntfy.example.com/inbound/{secret_path}` (one trailing slash is fine), and any other path gets the same HTTP 404 as a hostname with no <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">context</Terminology>. Works alongside `token`, but cannot be combined with `session`; see [Secret Paths](/docs/features/security#secret-paths). |

#### Session

| Field          | Type     | Required | Description |
| :------------- | :------- | :------- | :---------- |
| `secret`       | `string` | Yes      | Signing secret for this <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">context</Terminology>'s <Terminology title="A short-lived, one-use pass a sender gets from /session" to="/docs/reference/terminology#configuration">session tokens</Terminology>, at least 32 characters. The CLI generates a 64-character one. |
| `ttl_seconds`  | `number` | No       | How long a <Terminology title="A short-lived, one-use pass a sender gets from /session" to="/docs/reference/terminology#configuration">session token</Terminology> stays valid, a whole number from 60 to 3600 seconds. Defaults to 300. |
| `max_per_hour` | `number` | No       | How many <Terminology title="A short-lived, one-use pass a sender gets from /session" to="/docs/reference/terminology#configuration">session tokens</Terminology> one IP address can get per hour, a whole number from 1 to 1000. Defaults to 10. |

### Email Context

| Field               | Type       | Required | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| :------------------ | :--------- | :------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `id`                | `string`   | Yes      | Unique identifier. Must match the local part of the email address (e.g., `pfsense` for `pfsense@ntfy.example.com`). The CLI allows letters, digits, dots, underscores, and dashes; the config file accepts any non-empty ID. |
| `name`              | `string`   | Yes      | Human-readable name.                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `type`              | `"email"`  | Yes      | Must be `"email"` for email <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">contexts</Terminology>.                                                                       |
| `interpreter`       | `string`   | Yes      | Which <Terminology title="Turns one app's raw alert into a clean ntfy message" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">interpreter</Terminology> to use.                                                              |
| `topic`             | `string`   | Yes      | The ntfy <Terminology title="The ntfy channel your phone subscribes to for alerts" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">topic</Terminology> to publish to.                                                               |
| `error_topic`       | `string`   | No       | Optional <Terminology title="Where you hear about alerts the proxy couldn't handle" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">error topic</Terminology> for one-shot JSON diagnostics outside the durable lane.                                                |
| `error_events`      | `string[]` | No       | Filters which authentication and interpretation diagnostics notify `error_topic`. Any of `authentication`, `interpretation`. Omit for both (the default); e.g. `["interpretation"]` to mute authentication-failure noise. Dropped-notification notices always go to `error_topic` when it is set. |
| `mode`              | `string`   | Yes      | Delivery mode: `"send-once"` or `"send-all"`.                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `show_visitor_info` | `boolean`  | Yes      | For email <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">contexts</Terminology>, this setting has no effect — visitor details are only available for HTTP requests.      |
| `primary_server`    | `string`   | Yes      | Preferred <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">server</Terminology> name, which must also appear in `servers`.                                     |
| `servers`           | `string[]` | Yes      | Ordered <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">server</Terminology> name list.                                                                       |
| `allowed_from`      | `string` or `string[]` | No | Sender filter, as one entry or a list of entries. Each entry is an exact address (`user@example.com`) or a domain wildcard (`*@example.com`), and a sender that matches any entry passes. Case-insensitive. |

A list lets one email <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">context</Terminology> accept mail from several senders:

```json title="allowed_from as a list"
"allowed_from": ["*@githubstatus.com", "*@cloudflarestatus.com", "alerts@example.com"]
```

## Full Example

This example shows a complete `config.json` with two <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">servers</Terminology> and a mix of HTTP and email <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">contexts</Terminology>.

      "token": "tk_replace_with_fallback_token"

  "contexts": [
    {
      "id": "statuspage-example",
      "name": "GitHub Status",
      "type": "http",
      "interpreter": "statuspage",

      "error_topic": "errors",
      "error_events": ["interpretation"],
      "mode": "send-once",
      "show_visitor_info": false,
      "primary_server": "primary",
      "servers": ["primary", "fallback"],
      "secret_path": "replacewithageneratedsecretpathoflowercaselettersanddigits"
    },
    {
      "id": "seerr-example",
      "name": "Seerr Requests",
      "type": "http",
      "interpreter": "seerr",
      "topic": "media-requests",
      "mode": "send-once",
      "show_visitor_info": false,
      "primary_server": "primary",
      "servers": ["primary"],
      "token": "replace_with_auth_token"
    },
    {
      "id": "synology-example",
      "name": "Synology NAS",
      "type": "http",
      "interpreter": "synology",
      "topic": "nas-alerts",
      "mode": "send-once",
      "show_visitor_info": true,
      "primary_server": "primary",
      "servers": ["primary"],
      "token": "replace_with_auth_token"
    },
    {
      "id": "webhook-example",
      "name": "Generic Webhook",

      "interpreter": "plain-text",
      "topic": "webhooks",
      "mode": "send-once",
      "show_visitor_info": true,

        "secret": "replace_with_a_session_secret_of_32_or_more_characters"

    {
      "id": "pfsense",

      "type": "email",
      "interpreter": "pfsense",

    "base_domain": "ntfy.example.com",

## Validation

The CLI checks the config against its schema whenever it loads it. `nrp validate`, which deploy also runs, adds the reference and duplicate checks:

- **Structural** — All required fields are present and correctly typed, and a <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">context</Terminology> with a key the schema doesn't know fails.
- **<Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">Server</Terminology> references** — Every `primary_server` and every entry in `servers` must match an existing <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">server</Terminology> `name`, and each `primary_server` must also appear in its <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">context</Terminology>'s `servers`.
- **Duplicate <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">context</Terminology> IDs** — No two <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">contexts</Terminology> can share the same `id`.
- **HTTP context IDs** — IDs are valid DNS host labels: 1–63 lowercase alphanumeric or hyphen characters, with no leading or trailing hyphen.
- **Token or session** — An HTTP <Terminology title="One alert source: its address, interpreter, and topic" to="/docs/reference/terminology#how-it-works">context</Terminology> can set `token` or `session`, not both, and a session `secret` needs at least 32 characters.
- **Secret path** — A `secret_path` is 32 to 128 lowercase letters and digits, and it can't be set alongside `session`.
- **Allowed senders** — `allowed_from` is a non-empty string or a non-empty list of non-empty strings.
- **Token format** — <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">Server</Terminology> tokens must start with `tk_`.
- **URL format** — <Terminology title="One of your ntfy instances: a name, URL, and token" to="/docs/reference/terminology#ntfy-servers-and-messages">Server</Terminology> URLs must be valid and start with `https://`.
- **Settings format** — `worker_name` and `base_domain` must follow the formats in [Settings](#settings).

:::tip
Run validation manually with `nrp validate`, or choose **Deploy** in the interactive menu, which validates the config before it deploys anything.
:::
